# Do agentic LLMs learn to predict their tools?

*Independent review by GPT-6.2-astra, given raw data only.*

**Scaling Qwen3 gives the most consistent improvement. Terminal-agent SFT gives a narrower, measurable benefit.** On fresh, executed command/state pairs, TMax SFT raises plain-text binding accuracy from 71.7% to 75.3%, matching 14B instruct at 8B. Yet 8B base scores 77.1%, and asking for the same output in chat reverses the SFT advantage. On real software-engineering trajectories, TMax SFT also improves output likelihood, by the equivalent of a 1.31× increase in instruct-model size. Its subsequent RL step removes most of that gain. Other agent checkpoints range from modest improvements to substantial regressions. The evidence supports learning that helps with particular tool predictions. It offers little basis for attributing agent benchmark success generally to a better internal simulator.

## 2. Terminus measures imitation of both sides of a session

**Strong for the observed likelihood trade-off.** I summed negative log probabilities and bytes across 200 teacher-stratified held-out trajectories, excluding wrappers and dropping trailing messages beyond the 32k window, and selected each model’s temperature on 50 separate trajectories. At 8B, replacing instruct with Nemotron-Terminal increases neutral observation loss from .161 to .275 bits/byte. At their fitted temperatures the losses are .139 and .254, a difference of +.115 [+.105, +.126] in a paired trajectory bootstrap. The fitted penalty also appears with Qwen role markers and at 14B and 32B. On the very same neutral histories, Nemotron assigns the teacher’s thoughts **+.770** and actions **+.260** bits/token more log probability, while assigning observations **−.357**. This is a direct dissociation between writing like the agent and anticipating its terminal. The unit of prediction matters. A token counts as copied when its ending eight-token sequence occurred earlier, including earlier in the current response. This covers 43.4% of neutral observation tokens. At 8B the instruct→Nemotron losses are .015→.209 bits/byte on copies and .261→.321 on the remainder. “Novel” can still include a familiar shell prompt or a shorter copied string. In the token-class breakdown, shell prompts and command echoes occupy 48.2% of tokens. A controlled substitution reinforces the distinction. For repeated 16-character hex strings in a neutral real-trajectory prefix, instruct costs .023 bits/character and Nemotron 1.176. In a short synthetic terminal prefix the same comparison is .008 and .046. The penalty depends sharply on context. Even the apparent *gain* on native-format filtered content changes sign with calibration, from −.173 to +.055 bits/token at 8B. **Weak evidence for predicting errors.** Prompting for exact output moves it into the assistant role, still teacher-forced for up to 512 tokens. On 469 native-format items, Nemotron-8B then beats instruct, .117 versus .156 bits/byte. Only 44 items have a new error by the regex label. Nemotron’s Yes/No AUROC changes from .653 in neutral format to .475 in native format, where its mean probability on the two allowed answers is below .001. ## 3. SWE-rebench shows modest gains for one lineage and large losses for others

**Moderate for checkpoint differences, strong for the scale trend in the reported aggregates.** The sample contains 150 resolved runs from five teachers on 86 tasks in 66 repositories. For each teacher/task the builder favored the shortest successful run. Task dates in spring 2026 reduce exposure to the exact issues during pretraining, though the repositories are public. Calibration is separated by task. M1 scores teacher-forced content after dropping repeated eight-grams and classes such as whitespace, shell echoes and random hashes. Its headline code length estimates a rank-bucket probability conditional on model confidence, calibrated on other tasks, then charges for the width of the bucket. M2 scores eligible tokens in the first 512 tokens of the *same* observed response after the original command or two same-kind replacements, with the current thought removed. Its true-command loss uses a calibration-fitted temperature. ## 4. Executed counterfactuals reveal a format-dependent binding gain

**Strong on the fixed plain benchmark, moderate for transfer.** I aligned all 4,200 item IDs and used the 3,600 primary pairs, 900 per ordinary/harder computation and state-tracking stratum. Each pair changes a command or prior state. I recovered binding from the sign of crossed minus matched total loss, awarding half credit on ties. This cancels an output preference common to both contexts. One branch can carry the interaction even if the model would choose the same output in both contexts. The score includes an output terminator. Masked copied words remain visible as prefixes, and small integers and punctuation still earn credit. Same-family unrelated output pairs give 50.0% binding for 8B instruct. At temperature one with copied words masked, Nemotron beats size-matched instruct by **3.06, 3.42 and 2.94 points** at 8B, 14B and 32B. The first two are 1.73× and 2.08× on the instruct binding curve. The 32B score of 82.25% exceeds the ladder, so its multiplier is only bounded below by 1×. Against base, Nemotron-8B is **−2.36 points** [−3.97, −0.74] and Nemotron-14B is **+0.33** [−1.18, +1.81]. TMax SFT gains 3.58 points over instruct [1.89, 5.21], equivalent to 1.90×. Its RL increment is −0.42 [−1.50, +0.69]. Light-IF gains 1.47 points at 8B, while TMax SFT beats Light-IF by another 2.11 [0.44, 3.83]. CodeScout, Arctic-AWM, FrogMini and OpenThinkerAgent-32B are all within 0.6 points of instruct. The size comparison also depends on the metric. The generator retains unequal outputs distinguishable after masking. This tests discrimination on a selected grammar, with limited coverage of long programs and unseen repository state. ## 5. AgenticPPL rewards confident prediction of selected commands

**Strong for what the score measures, limited for what causes its benchmark correlation.** On 960 held-out Terminus trajectories the default score averages negative loss on 10,568 selected **action** ranges that a reference model could infer from earlier output. Token and range losses are capped at two bits. At 8B, base→instruct improves the score from −.929 to −.827 even as uncapped bits per picked range worsen from 1.807 to 3.224. Nemotron improves both, to −.773 and 1.441. TMax SFT→RL drops from −.877 to −.928 while its reported TB-Lite success rises from 11.5 to 17.7 (TB2.1 falls from 6.0 to 5.2). Across 20 checkpoints I obtain Spearman ρ = −.77 with action-token entropy. After subtracting size-group means, Pearson r is −.65 with entropy and −.10 with plain sandbox binding. The 0.6B implementation check differs from the reference engine by .00088 on the default score. The correlations implicate sharpness, while Nemotron’s uncapped improvement points to better command imitation as well. A useful rank predictor can exploit these without predicting tool outputs. This panel lacks uniform live benchmark scores to revalidate the original rank claim. ## Interpretation and decisive next test

**Mechanisms remain tentative.** Pretraining plausibly improves code and text continuation together. Policy training can reward choosing a useful command while supplying its output for free. CodeScout explicitly masks observation tokens in training. Nemotron’s mask is unknown. Role expectations, entropy changes, copied identifiers, checkpoint-specific forgetting, teacher style and task overlap could each explain part of the pattern. SWE additionally contains file contents a model may never have seen in context. None of these experiments identifies an internal state representation or demonstrates that changing it mediates task completion.

**Next experiment.** Run a blinded, paired replay of real repository tasks with the public Qwen3-8B base→instruct→TMax SFT→TMax RL lineage, Light-IF-8B and 14B references. Fork each snapshot, change one earlier edit or input, expose the causally relevant evidence in both histories, then execute the same test in both. Balance outcomes and match lengths and identifiers. Score the first distinguishing result before any revealing output prefix, in both plain transcript and assistant-answer formats. Predefine matched-versus-crossed binding and per-branch correct choice, a state-hidden control, and paired intervals by held-out repository. A gain over the parent across formats that disappears when the changed state is hidden would distinguish transferable state use from the format-sensitive recovery seen here.
